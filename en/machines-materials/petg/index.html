The material

What is PETG?

PETG is a variant of PET, the plastic used for bottles, modified with glycol to be easier to print and less brittle. It combines good strength, slight flexibility and good chemical resistance.

It is often the first accessible engineering material: it handles heat better than PLA (around 70–80 °C), resists many acids, salts and bases, and takes impacts better.

PETG comes in many colours, including translucent ones. We use it for electronic enclosures, containers, covers and indoor functional parts.

Pros / cons

Strengths & limits

Strengths
  • Resists acids, salts and bases
  • Little prone to shrinkage and stringing
  • Durable and slightly flexible
  • Wide choice of colours, translucent included
  • Better heat resistance than PLA
Limits
  • Surface slightly less fine than PLA
  • Average UV resistance for prolonged outdoor use
  • Less stiff than ABS or filled nylons

Designing your part in PETG

Design tips

  • For food contact, specify the use: compliance depends on the filament and the finished part.
  • Round off sharp corners to limit stress concentrations.

FAQ

PETG: frequently asked questions

PETG or PLA?

PLA for appearance and accuracy, PETG for a part that has to work: stronger, less brittle, more resistant to heat and chemicals.

Is PETG food-safe?

Some PETG filaments are certified for food contact; the compliance of a printed part also depends on its surface and its use.

Is PETG water-resistant?

Yes, it absorbs very little water and is suitable for parts exposed to moisture.

PETG: a versatile, tough filament for 3D printing

PETG is an essential material in the world of 3D printing . Known for its versatility, strength and superior resistance compared with PLA, it is widely used both by professionals and by users looking for more durable parts suited to technical uses.

PETG filament combines several advantages much sought after in additive manufacturing: good mechanical strength, excellent layer adhesion and better resistance to moisture and temperature changes. Thanks to these properties, it is an excellent choice for manufacturing functional parts, technical components, brackets, enclosures and items for everyday use.

One of the great strengths of PETG for 3D printers is its balance between ease of printing and technical performance. Stronger than PLA while easier to print than some industrial materials, it produces solid parts with a fine finish.

PETG 3D printing is also appreciated for its slight flexibility and good impact resistance, which reduces the risk of breakage on parts under moderate mechanical stress. Its moisture resistance also makes it an interesting material for certain applications in humid environments or moderate outdoor use.

Available in many colours and finishes, PETG can produce technical parts as well as visually refined creations. Some variants offer translucent, matte or technical effects suited to specific needs in prototyping and functional manufacturing.

At O&D Impression 3D , we regularly use high-quality PETG to produce technical parts, functional components and projects requiring better mechanical and thermal resistance than PLA. Its versatility and reliability make it a particularly popular material for professional applications and demanding projects.

Although PETG has many advantages, it requires more precise print settings than PLA and can produce more stringing depending on the settings used. The choice of material therefore always depends on the technical, mechanical and environmental constraints of the project.

Choosing a professional PETG filament makes it possible to obtain stronger, more durable prints suited to a wide range of applications in modern 3D printing.
