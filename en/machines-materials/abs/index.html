The material

What is ABS?

ABS (acrylonitrile butadiene styrene) is one of the most widespread engineering plastics in industry: appliance housings, interior car parts, building bricks. In 3D printing, it can produce parts very close to injection-moulded ones.

It is hard, impact-resistant and handles heat well (about 90–100 °C). It can be drilled, tapped, bonded and smoothed for a glossy look.

Printing it requires an enclosed, heated chamber to avoid shrinkage and cracks: that is the case with our Bambu Lab X1C, H2C and Prusa Core One printers.

Pros / cons

Strengths & limits

Strengths
  • High hardness and impact resistance
  • Resists heat and abrasion
  • Machinable, bondable, smoothable
  • High stiffness
  • Proven industrial material
Limits
  • UV-sensitive outdoors (prefer ASA)
  • Odour when printing (managed in an enclosed chamber)
  • May shrink on very large parts

  • For outdoor use, switch to ASA.

FAQ

ABS: frequently asked questions

ABS or ASA?

They are very similar. ASA resists UV and suits outdoor use; ABS is an excellent choice indoors.

Can an ABS part be smoothed?

Yes, ABS can be smoothed to get a glossy surface with no visible layers.

Can ABS handle the heat inside a car?

It holds up much better than PLA; for areas exposed to the sun, ASA is preferable.

Detailed descriptionEverything to know: click to expand

Widely used in industry, automotive and consumer electronics, ABS is the natural choice for technical enclosures, under-bonnet parts, functional prototypes and parts requiring a careful finish.

At O&D Impression 3D, we print ABS on machines with a heated enclosure to avoid warping and guarantee dimensional quality.

USE CASES: WHAT FOR?

  • Electronic and industrial enclosures
  • Car parts (interior, brackets)
  • High-fidelity functional prototypes
  • Parts requiring acetone smoothing
  • Manufacturing moulds and jigs
