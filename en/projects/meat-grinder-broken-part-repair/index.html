Customer project · Reverse engineering

Reproducing a broken part for a meat grinder

A customer brought us the faulty part from her electric meat grinder, unavailable as a spare part. In less than two hours, modelling included, the part was reproduced identically and her appliance was working again.

Project photos

Broken original part and faithful PLA reproduction

The project

A meat grinder out of action because of one unobtainable part

It is unfortunately a common situation: a household appliance breaks down because of a single faulty mechanical part, in this case a plastic gear at the heart of the grinder's transmission mechanism. Worn by years of use, the part was no longer available from the manufacturer or in shops. The only option seemed to be buying a new appliance.

Our customer preferred another route: bringing us the damaged part so that we could reproduce it. Starting from the physical part, our technician produced a complete 3D model of the gear. Dimensions, teeth, central bore: everything was redrawn precisely in about an hour of design work.

This is exactly the kind of job where on-demand 3D printing is irreplaceable: bringing an appliance back to life when its spare part no longer exists, quickly, economically and sustainably.

A common finding: for many household appliances, spare parts become impossible to find after a few years. 3D printing can reproduce them at low cost instead of throwing away the whole appliance.
Technical solution

Modelling + PLA printing in under 2 h

After modelling, printing was launched on our Prusa Core One, the latest FDM printer added to our fleet. The material chosen was PLA (polylactic acid): strong, with excellent dimensional accuracy and well suited to gears under moderate mechanical stress, as is the case in a household meat grinder.

The final result matches the original part in dimensions and geometry. The gear teeth mesh correctly with the other parts of the mechanism, and the appliance is working normally again.

Total lead time from receipt to delivery of the part: under 48 h. Cost: far lower than buying a new appliance.

What if it were your appliance?

Reverse engineering for private customers

This project shows a use of 3D printing that the general public often doesn't know about: reproducing unobtainable spare parts for everyday appliances. Meat grinders, food processors, vacuum cleaners, coffee machines, lawnmowers: gears, cams and plastic parts break and disappear from suppliers' catalogues. 3D printing can extend the life of your equipment at low cost.

Our process is simple: bring us the broken part (or a photo with approximate measurements), we assess feasibility, produce the 3D model and deliver the reproduced part. No CAD file needed, no technical expertise needed on your side.

Service available for private customers and businesses in the province of Liège and throughout Belgium. Our workshop is in Welkenraedt, 15 minutes from Verviers and 30 minutes from Liège.
