Our technologies

Three technologies, every need

FDM for technical parts, SLA for precision, SLS for free-form geometries.

Industrial FFF / FDM

Molten filament, layer by layer. PETG, nylon, ABS, polycarbonate, fibre composites. Build volumes up to 400 mm.

Stereolithography (SLA)

UV curing for extreme precision. Smooth finishes, fine detail, ideal for dental and jewellery.

SLS sintering

Nylon powder sintered by laser. No supports, free-form geometries, high mechanical strength.

Post-processing

Sanding, chemical smoothing, painting, tapping, metal inserts: finished parts, ready to use.

Small batch

From 1 to 500 identical parts with quality control. Unit price decreases with quantity.

Urgent jobs

Express service available for industrial emergencies. Guaranteed lead time by arrangement.

Comparison

Additive manufacturing vs traditional methods

3D printing generates no material waste and requires no tooling. Ideal for complex parts and small series.

  • No tooling required (vs €5,000–50,000 for a mould)
  • First part in 1–5 days (vs 4–12 weeks)
  • Free design changes (vs a new mould)
  • Ideal for 1 to 500 parts
  • Geometries impossible to machine become possible

FAQ

Questions about additive manufacturing

Which technology should I choose for my part?

FDM for everyday functional parts, resin for precision and aesthetics, SLS for complex geometries and small nylon series.

Can additive manufacturing replace CNC machining?

For complex parts with internal channels or organic geometries, 3D printing is often better. For very tight tolerances (<±0.05 mm), CNC remains more suitable. The two are often complementary.

What are your lead times for a series of 100 parts?

For a series of 100 standard parts, allow 5 to 7 working days. We plan production across several machines in parallel.

Do you provide material certificates?

Yes, on request. Material data sheets and a certificate of conformity are available for each batch.

Can you print heat-resistant parts?

Yes: ASA (90 °C), ABS (80 °C), PC (110 °C), PEEK (250 °C), high-temperature resin (200–220 °C). We select the material according to your operating temperature.

What happens if a part is defective?

We inspect every part before shipping. If a defect slips through and is noticed on delivery, we remake the part at no extra cost.

Materials & properties

Choosing the right material for your application

Additive manufacturing only performs well if the material is perfectly suited to the intended use.

Standard vs engineering materials

Standard materials such as PLA are suitable for visual prototypes and non-functional parts. For industrial applications, we work with engineering materials: PETG-CF (carbon), nylon PA12, polycarbonate, UV-resistant ASA and glass-fibre-reinforced composites.

Each material has its advantages: mechanical strength, heat resistance, flexibility, chemical resistance. We guide you to the best choice for your specific constraints.

The importance of print settings

The final strength of a 3D-printed part depends as much on the print settings as on the material: infill rate, infill pattern, layer thickness, build orientation. The same part can have very different mechanical properties depending on these settings.

Our technician systematically optimises these settings for every order. For critical parts, we carry out strength tests on test specimens before starting final production.
