PA12-CF vs PA12-GF: carbon fibre or glass fibre?

PA12-CF (carbon-fibre nylon) and PA12-GF (glass-fibre nylon) share the same nylon base, but their reinforcements give them very different behaviours. This detailed comparison helps you make the right choice for your industrial application.

Short carbon fibre gives the nylon matrix exceptional stiffness and lightness. Ideal for structural parts where weight is a key constraint. Critical point: it is brittle under point impacts, so avoid it for any part likely to take direct hits.

PA12-GF (glass)

Stiffness78%
Impact resist.62%
Lightness55%
Dim. stability88%
Heat resist.78%
Surface finish32%

Glass fibre offers a more balanced reinforcement: less stiff than carbon fibre, but much more impact-resistant and less brittle. It is often the best choice for industrial parts subject to dynamic loads or continuous vibration.

FAQ

PA12-CF vs PA12-GF: frequently asked questions

Are these materials compatible with standard FDM printers?

No. PA12-CF and PA12-GF are abrasive and require hardened steel nozzles and high-temperature extruders. On a consumer printer with a brass nozzle, these materials destroy it within a few hours. Our machines are equipped accordingly.

Can the parts be machined or drilled after printing?

Yes, both materials can be drilled, milled and tapped. Use tools suited to composites. For critical threads, we recommend embedding metal inserts during printing.

What is the price difference between PA12-CF and PA12-GF?

Carbon fibre is generally 20 to 40% more expensive than glass fibre as a raw material. On the final quote, the difference also depends on the volume of material. Ask for a comparative quote for your specific part.

Do these materials resist oils and solvents?

The PA12 nylon base offers good resistance to mineral oils, greases and aliphatic solvents. It resists concentrated acids and chlorinated solvents poorly. Tell us which chemicals will be in contact so we can validate before production.

Is the rough surface of composites a problem?

For functional applications (mechanical parts, jigs), roughness is generally not a problem. For applications that need a smooth surface, post-processing by sanding followed by a primer is possible, but costly on such hard materials.

Can PA12-CF or PA12-GF be used in SLS?

PA12-GF is available in an SLS version in our catalogue (sls-pa12-charge-verre). SLS PA12-GF offers support-free geometries impossible in FDM, with even better mechanical strength. Contact us to compare the two processes for your application.

Good to know

Fibre composites in 3D printing: why fibre changes everything

Adding short fibres to a polymer matrix is one of the most significant innovations of recent years in FDM 3D printing. The principle is simple: microscopic carbon or glass fibres are mixed into the PA12 nylon filament before extrusion. During printing, these fibres partly align in the deposition direction, creating a directional reinforcement effect that drastically improves the stiffness and mechanical strength of the finished part.

PA12-CF (short carbon fibre) is the stiffest composite we offer in FDM. Its Young's modulus, the measure of its stiffness, is two to three times higher than that of pure PA12 nylon. It is also lighter than glass fibre, making it the preferred choice for aerospace, motorsport and any equipment where the strength-to-weight ratio is critical. On the other hand, carbon fibres make it more brittle under impact: a PA12-CF part fractures cleanly rather than deforming gradually.

PA12-GF (short glass fibre) offers a more balanced compromise. Its stiffness is lower than carbon, but its impact resistance is clearly better. Glass fibres are less abrasive than carbon fibres, which makes them slightly less aggressive for print nozzles, although both our materials require hardened steel nozzles. For industrial parts subject to continuous vibration, repeated impacts or dynamic loads, PA12-GF is generally our first recommendation.

An important technical point for both materials: print orientation has a significant influence on the final mechanical properties. The fibres align in the extrusion direction, which creates anisotropy: the part will be stronger in the XY direction than in the Z direction (between layers). Our technician systematically analyses your part to determine the optimal orientation according to the main stress axes of your application.

Both materials are available at O&D Impression 3D in FDM, and PA12-GF is also available in SLS (laser sintering) in our SLS material catalogue. The SLS version offers even better mechanical properties thanks to the isotropy of the process: SLS parts have almost identical properties in all directions, which is impossible in FDM.
