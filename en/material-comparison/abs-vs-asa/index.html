ABS vs ASA: which engineering plastic for your project?

ABS and ASA share very similar mechanical properties but differ on one crucial point: UV resistance. This comparison helps you choose between these two engineering materials available in our Welkenraedt workshop.

ABS remains excellent for indoor technical parts. Its big advantage: acetone smoothing gives near-perfect surfaces. Its weak point is a major one: exposed to UV, it yellows quickly and becomes brittle. Reserve it for indoor use only.

ASA

Mech. strength76%
Heat resist.72%
UV resistance95%
Ease of printing52%
Acetone smoothing60%
Cost58%

ASA was developed specifically for outdoor applications. Its mechanical properties are almost identical to ABS, but its resistance to UV and weathering is incomparably better. The extra cost over ABS is fully justified for any outdoor use.

Verdict

Our workshop recommendation

Recommended by default

Choose ABS if…

The part stays indoors, you need acetone smoothing, and budget matters. ABS is the reference for indoor technical parts up to ~100°C.

⬆ Move up a grade if…

ASA

When in doubt, choose ASA. It does everything ABS does, and does it better outdoors. The price difference is minimal compared with the long-term durability gained.

PA12-CF / PEEK

If your part exceeds 100°C or needs truly high mechanical strength, ABS and ASA reach their limits. See our high-performance materials.

FAQ

ABS vs ASA: frequently asked questions

Can ASA be smoothed with acetone like ABS?

ASA is partially soluble in acetone, but the result is less perfect than with ABS. For a smooth finish on ASA, we prefer progressive sanding followed by a spray primer. For optimal chemical smoothing, ABS remains superior.

How long does ASA resist UV?

ASA is formulated to withstand UV for 5 to 10 years in normal outdoor conditions, without significant discolouration or embrittlement. That is why it is used in the automotive industry and outdoor electronics.

Are ABS and ASA dangerous to print?

Both release volatile compounds during printing, including styrene for ABS. In our workshop, all ABS/ASA prints are made in closed enclosures with filtration. For workshop use, good ventilation is essential.

Can ABS and ASA be painted?

Yes, both take acrylic paint and lacquer very well after a suitable primer. ASA takes automotive body paints particularly well, which makes it a popular choice for vehicle parts.

ABS or ASA for 3D-printed drone parts?

For drones used outdoors, ASA is the obvious choice: resistance to UV, weather and temperature changes. If the drone is only used indoors, ABS is suitable and cheaper. In both cases, a high infill (40–60%) is recommended for impact resistance.

Good to know

ABS and ASA in 3D printing: everything you need to know before ordering

ABS (acrylonitrile butadiene styrene) is one of the best-known engineering plastics in the world. Used for decades in the automotive industry, consumer electronics and toy manufacturing, it was one of the first materials adopted by FDM 3D printing. Its heat resistance up to 100°C, its rigidity and the possibility of chemical smoothing with acetone make it a popular choice for indoor functional parts.

ASA (acrylonitrile styrene acrylate) appeared later as a direct answer to the limitations of ABS against UV and weathering. Originally developed for exterior automotive applications (mirrors, bumper trims, signage housings), ASA offers mechanical and thermal properties almost identical to ABS but with incomparably better UV resistance. It does not yellow, does not become brittle and keeps its mechanical properties after years of sun exposure.

In our Welkenraedt workshop, we print ABS and ASA in closed enclosures with controlled chamber temperature. This is essential for these materials, which are very sensitive to temperature variations during printing: a draught is enough to cause delamination or warping (lifting from the build plate). On unsuitable open-source machines these materials are hard to master; in a professional workshop we get repeatable, consistent results.

For Belgian companies with parts exposed outdoors, such as road signs, agricultural equipment, security system housings or street-lighting brackets, ASA is systematically our recommendation. The marginal extra cost over ABS is largely offset by longer durability and no maintenance due to yellowing or embrittlement.

A lesser-known but very effective use of ASA: motorsport car parts. Several motorsport teams in the region use our service to produce ASA guards, aerodynamic covers and trim parts exposed to UV and to large temperature differences between the inside and outside of the vehicle.
