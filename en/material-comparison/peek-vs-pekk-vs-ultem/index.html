PEEK vs PEKK vs Ultem: which high-performance polymer?

PEEK, PEKK and Ultem (PEI) are the three high-performance polymers we offer on our INTAMSYS Funmat HT. Reserved for extreme applications (aerospace, medical, heavy industry), here is how to choose between these exceptional materials.

FAQ

Questions about high-performance polymers

Do these materials require a special quote?

Yes. PEEK, PEKK and Ultem are quoted individually. The price depends heavily on the volume of material, the geometry and the certified grade required. Expect 5 to 10 times the price of an equivalent PETG part.

Are these materials available with fibre reinforcement?

Yes. We offer PEEK/CF, PEEK/GF, PEKK/CF and PEKK/GF. These composite grades combine the thermal and chemical properties of these polymers with the mechanical reinforcement of fibres. The composite grades perform even better, and cost more.

Can PEEK parts be autoclave-sterilised?

Medical-grade PEEK can be autoclave-sterilised (134°C, pressurised steam) repeatedly without degradation. It is one of the reasons it is used in surgery and implantology. Always specify the grade required for your medical application.

How do I know whether my application really needs PEEK?

The general rule: if your part works below 150°C and is not exposed to concentrated acids, PA12 or ASA is often enough. PEEK is justified when no other polymer can withstand the conditions of use. Good to know

High-performance polymers: when standard plastics are no longer enough

The vast majority of 3D printing projects do not need high-performance polymers. PLA, PETG or PA12 cover 95% of everyday needs. But there are cases where none of these materials is enough: continuous temperatures above 150°C, prolonged contact with concentrated acids, autoclave sterilisation requirements, fire-resistance certifications in aerospace. It is precisely for these extreme applications that we offer PEEK, PEKK and Ultem.

PEEK (polyether ether ketone) is probably the best-known polymer in the high-performance field. Used since the 1980s in the aerospace, oil and medical industries, it can operate continuously up to 300°C and resist a very wide range of aggressive chemicals. In 3D printing, it requires an extrusion temperature of 380 to 420°C and a chamber heated to 150–200°C, settings that only our INTAMSYS Funmat HT can reach in our workshop.

PEKK (polyether ketone ketone) belongs to the same family of semi-crystalline aromatic polymers. It offers better interlayer adhesion than PEEK, which results in mechanically more uniform parts. Its service temperature range is slightly lower (up to 250°C), but it is often preferred in aerospace and defence for its better printability and superior dynamic properties.

Ultem (PEI: polyetherimide) differs from the other two in one unique and decisive feature: its native UL94 V0 flame-retardant certification. It is the only one of the three to meet, without additional treatment, the fire-resistance standards imposed by aviation regulation FAR 25.853 (depending on grade). It is widely used to manufacture cabin interior parts, avionics brackets and enclosures for certified industrial electronics.
